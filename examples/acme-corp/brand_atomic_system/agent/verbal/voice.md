# Voice & Tone

## Voice (consistent across all contexts)

Acme Corp sounds like a senior engineer who learned to write clearly. We are direct, honest, and technically credible without being condescending. We assume the reader is smart and busy.

**Core voice attributes:**
- **Direct** — We say what we mean. No weasel words, no vague superlatives.
- **Concrete** — Abstract claims get backed by specifics. Not "fast" but "< 50ms median delivery."
- **Peer-to-peer** — We're talking to builders, as a builder. Not brand-to-customer.
- **Confident but not arrogant** — We know our lane and stay in it.

## Tone (shifts by context)

| Context | Tone | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Docs | Instructional, patient | "Call the `/events` endpoint with your payload. Here's what each field does." |
| Marketing | Aspirational, grounded | "Stop writing retry logic. Your product deserves the time back." |
| Error messages | Honest, helpful | "This webhook failed because the endpoint returned 429. We'll retry in 30s." |
| Social | Warmer, slightly playful | "Turns out the real SLAs were the friends we made along the way. Just kidding — here are the numbers." |

## Do / Don't

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Use specific numbers and examples | Make vague claims ("best-in-class," "powerful") |
| Acknowledge tradeoffs honestly | Oversell or hide limitations |
| Write for skimmers (headers, short paragraphs) | Write dense walls of text |
| Use "you" and "your team" | Use "users" (impersonal) |
| Active voice | Passive voice when avoidable |
| Celebrate the builder, not the brand | Make Acme Corp the hero |
